# Stronger baselines for Table V — PID and reference-rate feedforward

2026-09-10. All runs: MuJoCo, 45 s, dt = 0.5 ms, Table V flags exactly
(`run_campaign.py`: β = 0.5, η = 8e-4, k_e = 8, k_ω* = 0, CF2 actuator
scale 1.5). Mean quaternion-angle error θ̄_q [rad]. One run per cell (the
missions are deterministic; the paper's noise table already shows seed
spread of 0.002–0.031%).

## Headline

**The PD in eq. (11) damps absolute body rate, `−k_ω⊙ω`, not the rate error
`ω_e = ω − R(q_e)ᵀω_d` that eq. (6) defines.** On a moving reference that
leaves a velocity lag. Fixing it — PD with reference-rate feedforward,
`−k_ω⊙(ω − ω_d)`, no integral and no learning — removes 76–99% of PD's error
on the four moving-reference missions and beats CMG–PES there by 2–17×.

**CMG–PES remains complementary.** Added on top of that stronger baseline it
cuts error by a further **46.3–80.2%** on the moving-reference missions and
**18.4–21.0%** on Hardstep, where feedforward cannot help — every one of the
ten cells improves, with a *smaller* residual than on plain PD and no added
saturation.

## PID (what was originally asked)

- Integral gain as a multiple of k_q per axis (k_q ∝ inertia; the airframes
  differ by ~10³ in inertia, so one absolute k_i cannot be fair to both).
- Grid 0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1, 2. **Best single factor 0.05 on
  both platforms, an interior optimum.** Tuned on the evaluation missions,
  which favours the baseline.
- At 0.05: mission-mean error improves only 1.6% (X2) / 2.0% (CF2) over PD.
  Yaw360 improves 45%; the other four get 0–2% worse.
- CMG on top of PID(0.05): 18.8–57.5% better than PID on all ten cells.
- **Per-mission tuning** (an oracle — nobody retunes per mission): PID beats
  CMG on Roll (~2×) and Yaw360 (~12×) with factor 2, but the same high gains
  **diverge on Hardstep** (2.2–2.5 rad, saturation up to 0.97) and on CF2
  Hard45. This persists with rotor-level anti-windup, so it is a property of
  high-gain integral action here, not an anti-windup artifact. No single
  integral gain serves the whole mission set.
- PID is dominated by PD+ff on every moving-reference mission anyway.

## How the feedforward was built, and checked

`ω_d` = central finite difference of `reference_quat` (h = 1e-4). Frame and
sign were **selected empirically**, because this codebase uses a passive
quaternion convention and a silent convention mismatch has broken a
controller here before. Four modes were run on X2 only; `left-neg`
(`ω_d = −2·vec(q(t+h)⊗q(t−h)⁻¹)/Δ`) reduced error on all four missions and
dominated on the two multi-axis ones. **Held-out check on CF2 — not used in
selection — passed on all four missions.**

## What this means for the paper

1. "Well-tuned PD" and the limitation sentence "the comparison is against a
   well-tuned PD without integral action" should go: the stronger omission is
   the rate feedforward, and a reviewer who reads eq. (6) next to eq. (11)
   will ask for it.
2. **Minimal change (fits the page budget):** keep Table V, add PD+ff and
   CMG|PD+ff columns (or one compact table), one paragraph, and state the
   feedforward law. The contribution survives and arguably strengthens:
   *18.4–80.2% over a feedforward PD baseline that itself removes 76–99% of
   PD's moving-reference error.*
3. **Full rebase:** rerun the noise table, disturbance study, and neuron-family
   benchmark on PD+ff. Compute is a few hours at 4 workers; the cost is
   rewriting figures and text. A scope decision for the advisors.
