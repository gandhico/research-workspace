\begin{abstract}
Online neuromorphic residual control can augment a classical attitude controller without changing its feedback gains or actuator interface. Six continuous coupled membrane-gate (CMG) units and eighteen decoder weights adapted by prescribed error sensitivity (PES) add a bounded correction to the saturated quaternion proportional-derivative (PD) torque request before rotor-force allocation. Across ten matched 45-s MuJoCo cases on the Skydio X2 and a Crazyflie~2 derived model, mean quaternion-angle error falls by 17.3--60.6\% (53--57\% on the Full3 and Hard45 multi-axis missions). The same units with a fixed decoder give at most 10.3\%; their internal signals attribute the gain to decoder adaptation rather than to the neuron dynamics alone. Against LIF, ALIF, Izhikevich, and LIFRate bases, CMG--PES attains better tracking at substantially lower realized residual effort. The reductions persist under synthetic measurement noise, and learned corrections are reused across repeated disturbances. PD remains a nominal, interpretable reference; the residual adds bounded, revocable adaptation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Learning-based augmentation can retain classical feedback while adapting a correction that is difficult to specify \emph{a priori}. We study a bounded torque residual for quaternion attitude tracking of an unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) with individual rotor-force saturation. The question is whether a small stateful basis improves a fixed PD controller, and whether any improvement comes from its neuron dynamics, decoder learning, or correction magnitude. Bounded authority makes the learning layer switchable; it does not by itself ensure stability or safety.

Prior work includes residual robot learning \cite{johannink2019}, geometric $\mathcal L_1$ quadrotor augmentation with performance guarantees \cite{wu2025l1quad}, and proportional--integral--derivative (PID) attitude control with a proximal policy optimization (PPO) residual \cite{li2027hpappo}. Neuromorphic control offers a complementary implementation: Nengo supports the Neural Engineering Framework (NEF) and online decoder learning \cite{bekolay2014nengo}, most often through PES, an established local, online error-driven update \cite{voelker2015pes}. PES-based adaptive control has been demonstrated on manipulators \cite{dewolf2016arm,dewolf2020nengo} and in a Nengo spiking PID controller for a free-flying UAV \cite{olivares2026selftuning}. We focus instead on a continuous membrane-gate basis for a bounded quaternion-torque residual, with a fixed classical controller and adaptation confined to its decoder.

We augment a quaternion PD baseline with coupled conductance-based membrane-gate (CMG) units and PES decoder learning. The paper's contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item a code-aligned CMG--PES architecture with explicit paired-current encoding, state-update, and readout equations and a fully specified actuator interface (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch});
    \item proofs of internal-state, command-deviation, and weight boundedness, exact PD recovery at zero authority, and an allocation-aware local stability condition;
    \item a controlled ablation separating conductance modulation, decoder learning, and output authority, which traces the gain to decoder adaptation through the membrane, gate, activity, residual, and decoder signals of matched fixed- and adaptive-decoder runs; and
    \item robustness evidence under measurement noise and a hold-equilibrium study in which the learned correction is reused across repeated disturbances.
\end{enumerate}

  %\item a matched five-mission, two-model study in which the direct and Nengo realizations agree to within $2.013\times10^{-6}$ in the maximum relative mean quaternion-angle tracking error difference across the ten missions;

The CMG unit couples a leaky membrane to a gating state that modulates its conductance, linking LIF-like integration with Hodgkin--Huxley inspired gating; the controller uses its continuous, non-spiking trajectory (related regimes can spike) as a compact set of stateful features. The implementation is digital and claims no neuromorphic-hardware energy advantage.

\section{Attitude Baseline and Actuator Interface}
\label{sec:problem}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The objective is to reduce shortest-path attitude error for a prescribed
$q_d(t)$ while respecting rotor-force limits. Baseline and augmented
controllers share the state measurements, height loop, and allocator;
Section~\ref{sec:architecture} modifies only the requested attitude torque.

%\input{figure1_interface.tex}

\subsection{Quaternion Convention and Error}
Let $Q\in\mathbb S^3$ denote the scalar-first Hamilton quaternion representing attitude (body to inertial), and let $\bm\omega$ be the body angular velocity. For a rigid body with inertia $\bm J\succ0$ about the center of mass, expressed in body axes (cf.~Fig.~\ref{fig:models}), the free-flight rotational dynamics are \cite{schaub2018amss}
\begin{align}
 \dot Q&=\tfrac12 Q\otimes[0,\bm\omega\T]\T,\\
 \bm J\dot{\bm\omega}&=\bm\tau_{\rm act}+\bm\tau_{\rm p}
       +\bm\tau_{\rm d}-\bm\omega\times(\bm J\bm\omega).
 \label{eq:plant}
\end{align}
Here $\bm\tau_{\rm p}$ is modeled passive torque and $\bm\tau_{\rm d}$ any applied disturbance, both zero in the nominal model; contact, if present, adds an external wrench.

We work with the corresponding passive quaternion $q=Q^{-1}$ \cite{diebel2006quaternion}, which satisfies $\dot q=-\tfrac12[0,\bm\omega\T]\T\otimes q$; the desired attitude $q_d$ uses the same convention. Define the attitude error
\begin{align}
 q_e&=q_d\otimes q^{-1}=[q_{e,0},\bm q_{e,v}\T]\T,\\
 \bm e_q&=\sgnp(q_{e,0})\bm q_{e,v},\qquad \sgnp(0)=1,\\
 \theta_q&=2\operatorname{atan2}(\|\bm q_{e,v}\|_2,|q_{e,0}|)
             \in[0,\pi].
 \label{eq:theta}
\end{align}
The reported $\theta_q$ is the shortest rotation angle, invariant under $q_e\mapsto-q_e$; sign selection introduces a discontinuity at $q_{e,0}=0$ and does not establish robust hybrid global stability \cite{mayhew2011hybrid}.

For a desired-frame rate $\bm\omega_d$ satisfying the corresponding passive kinematics, differentiation gives
\begin{align}
 \dot q_e&=\tfrac12q_e\otimes[0,\bm\omega_e\T]\T,\\
 \bm\omega_e&=\bm\omega-R(q_e)\T\bm\omega_d,\qquad
 \dot q_{e,0}=-\tfrac12\bm q_{e,v}\T\bm\omega_e.
 \label{eq:errkin}
\end{align}
These kinematic identities imply no convergence; reference Euler triples parameterize $q_d$ in this convention.

\subsection{Physical Inertia and Implemented PD Gains}
When principal moments $\bm j_{\rm p}$ of $\bm J$ are specified in a rotated principal-axis frame, the body-frame tensor is $\bm J=R_{BI}\operatorname{diag}(\bm j_{\rm p})R_{BI}\T$ for the rotation $R_{BI}$ from principal to body axes. The implementation sets axiswise gains from the principal moments in their reported ordering, $\bm j_{\rm tune}$, not necessarily $\operatorname{diag}(\bm J)$ (Table~\ref{tab:models}).

The fixed quaternion PD baseline is
\begin{align}
 \bm k_q&=2\bm j_{\rm tune}\odot\bm\omega_n^2,\qquad
 \bm k_\omega=2\zeta\bm j_{\rm tune}\odot\bm\omega_n,\\
 \bm u_0&=\sat_{\bm\ell}
       (-\bm k_q\odot\bm e_q-\bm k_\omega\odot\bm\omega),
 \label{eq:pd}
\end{align}
where $\odot$ denotes componentwise multiplication,
$\bm\omega_n=(4,4,3.5)\T$ rad/s, $\zeta=0.95$, and
$\sat_{\bm\ell}$ clips component $i$ to $[-\ell_i,\ell_i]$.
%
The componentwise torque limits $\bm\ell\in\R_{>0}^3$ define the requested-torque box $\mathcal U=\prod_{i=1}^3[-\ell_i,\ell_i]$, with
$\ell_i=\max(j_{{\rm tune},i}\nu_i,10^{-6}\,\mathrm{N\,m})$ and acceleration scale $\bm\nu=(12,12,10)\T$ rad/s$^2$; these controller-side bounds are not the rotor-feasible torque set. $\clip_{[a,b]}$ also acts componentwise, and $\oslash$ is componentwise division.
The law is quaternion feedback \cite{fresk2013quaternion}; $\bm\omega_n$ is a tuning parameter, not a set of coupled closed-loop natural frequencies. Desired-rate feedforward and gyroscopic cancellation are omitted. The gains are vehicle-specific through $\bm j_{\rm tune}$ (with $\bm\omega_n,\zeta$ shared), identical for PD and its CMG augmentation, and used unchanged, not as a scalar-gain surrogate, in Section~\ref{sec:local}.
%
\subsection{Requested and Realized Wrench}
For rotor positions $\bm p_i$ in body coordinates, parallel $+z$ thrust
axes, and signed reaction coefficients $\kappa_i$, define
\begin{equation}
 B=\begin{bmatrix}
 1&1&1&1\\p_{1,y}&p_{2,y}&p_{3,y}&p_{4,y}\\
 -p_{1,x}&-p_{2,x}&-p_{3,x}&-p_{4,x}\\
 \kappa_1&\kappa_2&\kappa_3&\kappa_4
 \end{bmatrix}.
 \label{eq:allocationmatrix}
\end{equation}
The signs follow $\bm p_i\times[0,0,f_i]\T$ and the local reaction torque $\kappa_i f_i$ ($\kappa_i$ in meters); $B$ maps rotor thrusts to total thrust and body torque. For collective thrust $T$ and body-torque command $\bm u$, inverse allocation with clipping is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \bm f_{\rm req}&=B^{-1}[T,\bm u\T]\T,\\
 \bm f_{\rm act}&=\clip_{[\bm0,\bm f_{\max}]}(\bm f_{\rm req}),\\
 [T_{\rm act},\bm\tau_{\rm act}\T]\T&=B\bm f_{\rm act}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:mixer}
\end{equation}
Here $\bm u=\bm u_0$ for PD; the CMG path (Section~\ref{sec:architecture})
modifies this request before the same allocation. Each $f_i$ is a commanded
force (N), not a rotor-speed command, and the wrench is realized exactly
only if $B^{-1}[T,\bm u\T]\T\in[\bm0,\bm f_{\max}]$, a
collective-dependent set that need not contain a componentwise torque box;
allocation is clipped inverse, not optimization-based
\cite{monteiro2016allocation}.

Collective thrust remains classical, with $z_d=1$ m:
\begin{equation}
 T=\clip_{[0,2.5Mg]}
 \left(\frac{M[g+6(z_d-z)-4\dot z]}{\max(R(Q)_{33},0.35)}\right).
 \label{eq:collective}
\end{equation}
Here $g$ is gravity; the loop reads altitude and vertical velocity, and the
denominator compensates tilt. The guard $0.35$ avoids near-singular
compensation as $R(Q)_{33}\to0$, limiting it to about
$\arccos(0.35)\approx69.5^\circ$; the upper clip does not supersede
individual rotor limits. The CMG path has no collective output but can
affect height through attitude, tilt compensation, and allocation
saturation.

\section{CMG State Dynamics and Online Decoder Adaptation}
\label{sec:architecture}
\label{sec:analysis}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Encoding, States, and Numerical Update}

For $m$ torque channels and $2m$ units, normalize the clipped baseline and
form positive/negative current pairs:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \bm I^{\pm}&=I_b\bm1+I_g\max(\pm\bar{\bm u},\bm0),\quad
 \bar{\bm u}=\bm u_0\oslash\bm\ell,\\
 \bm I&=[(\bm I^+)\T,(\bm I^-)\T]\T.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:encoding}
\end{equation}
Each unit has a dimensionless membrane state $v_j$ and gate $r_j$:
\begin{align}
 \dot r_j&=[\sigma(\alpha_h(v_j-v_{\rm th}+\gamma r_j))-r_j]/\tau_r,\\
 \dot v_j&=-(v_j-v_{\rm rest})/\tau+I_j/\tau-c_j(v_j-v_{\rm reset}),\\
 c_j&=(g_0+\chi g_1|I_j|)r_j^2,\qquad \chi\in\{0,1\}.
 \label{eq:cmg}
\end{align}
% [v6] CMG unit coupling topology (TikZ source under architectures/tikz/).
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\resizebox{0.86\columnwidth}{!}{\input{architectures/tikz/archB1c.tex}}
\caption{Coupling topology of one CMG unit, cf.~\eqref{eq:cmg}: the
membrane sets the gate target, and the gate shifts its own threshold

dependent if $\chi=1$). The dynamics are continuous, with no spike or
reset; only $a_j$ leaves the unit.}
\label{fig:cmgunit}
\end{figure}
%
The logistic gate target depends on $v_j$, while $r_j$ shifts that target
and modulates membrane conductance (Fig.~\ref{fig:cmgunit}), so both states
participate although the decoder reads only the gate-derived activity.
There is no threshold-triggered reset or spike; $v_{\rm reset}$ is a
reversal level in a continuous conductance term, and $g_0,g_1$ make $c_j$
a rate.

Integration is sequential, not exponential Euler for both states: from
the old states compute
\begin{align}
 b_{j,k}&=\sigma\!\left(\clip_{[-60,60]}
       [\alpha_h(v_{j,k}-v_{\rm th}+\gamma r_{j,k})]\right),\\
 r_{j,k}^{+}&=\clip_{[0,1]}
       [r_{j,k}+(h/\tau_r)(b_{j,k}-r_{j,k})].
 \label{eq:gate_step}
\end{align}
Then evaluate $c_{j,k}=(g_0+\chi g_1|I_{j,k}|)(r_{j,k}^{+})^2$,
$\lambda_{j,k}=\tau^{-1}+c_{j,k}$, and
$d_{j,k}=(v_{\rm rest}+I_{j,k})/\tau+c_{j,k}v_{\rm reset}$. The
frozen-coefficient membrane step is
\begin{equation}
 v_{j,k}^{+}=e^{-\lambda_{j,k}h}v_{j,k}
       +(1-e^{-\lambda_{j,k}h})d_{j,k}/\lambda_{j,k}.
 \label{eq:mem_step}
\end{equation}
It exactly solves the scalar membrane equation with frozen coefficients,
without implying unconditional stability of the coupled closed loop; then
$(v_{j,k+1},r_{j,k+1})=(v_{j,k}^{+},r_{j,k}^{+})$.

\subsection{Readout, PES, and Additive Residual}
The within-step activity, initial decoder, and clipped readout are
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \bm a_k&=\clip_{[0,1]}(G\bm r_k^+),\qquad W_0=[I_m,-I_m],\\
 \bm s_k&=\clip_{[-1,1]}(W_k\bm a_k).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:readout}
\end{equation}
Pair subtraction retains direction from a nonnegative basis, though state
memory does not imply instantaneous sign agreement after a command
reversal. With $G=10^5$ the activity saturates once $r_j\ge10^{-5}$, so
$G$ sets how much of $r_j$'s range reaches the decoder. The teaching
target and local update are
\begin{align}
 \bm y_k^*&=\clip_{[-1,1]}(-k_e\bm e_{q,k}-k_\omega^*\bm\omega_k),\quad
 \bm\varepsilon_k=\bm y_k^*-\bm s_k,\\
 W_{k+1}&=\Pi_{\mathcal W}
       [W_k+\eta\bm\varepsilon_k\bm a_k\T-\lambda_W W_k].
 \label{eq:pes}
\end{align}
Here $\mathcal W=[-W_{\max},W_{\max}]^{m\times2m}$, and $\eta,\lambda_W$
are per step (changing $h$ changes the learning dynamics). PES moves
toward the prescribed target $\bm y_k^*$; it does not differentiate
through the plant or minimize a closed-loop cost.

Using $W_k$ before the update, the requested command is
\begin{equation}
 \bm u_k=\sat_{\bm\ell}(\bm u_{0,k}+\beta\bm\ell\odot\bm s_k),\quad
 \bm\delta_k=\bm u_k-\bm u_{0,k},\quad 0\le\beta\le1.
 \label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
The canonical UAV case has $m=3$, six CMG units, and eighteen decoder
weights. Intrinsic conductance modulation ($\chi$), synaptic/decoder
adaptation ($W_k$), and output authority ($\beta\bm\ell$) are distinct.

\subsection{Structural Properties and Their Scope}
\begin{proposition}[Bounded internal states]
\label{prop:states}
With the nonnegative conductance parameters in Table~\ref{tab:parameters},
$r_{j,0}\in[0,1]$, and $0<h\le\tau_r$, the gate remains in $[0,1]$.
The membrane iterate remains in the convex hull of its initial value,
$v_{\rm reset}$, and the interval
$[v_{\rm rest}+I_b,v_{\rm rest}+I_b+I_g]$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The unclipped gate step is a convex combination of $r_{j,k}$ and
$b_{j,k}\in[0,1]$. Since $I_j\in[I_b,I_b+I_g]$ and $c_j\ge0$,
$d_j/\lambda_j$ is a convex combination of $v_{\rm rest}+I_j$ and
$v_{\rm reset}$, and \eqref{eq:mem_step} one of $v_{j,k}$ and
$d_j/\lambda_j$; induction proves both statements.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Bounded authority and baseline recovery]
\label{prop:authority}
For $\bm u_{0,k}\in\mathcal U$ and any finite $W_k$,
$\bm u_k\in\mathcal U$ and $|\delta_{k,i}|\le\beta\ell_i$.
If $\beta=0$, the requested baseline command is recovered at every step.
If $W_k=0$ at a particular step, it is recovered at that step, but learning
need not keep $W_{k+1}=0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The outer clip enforces membership in $\mathcal U$. Its componentwise
nonexpansiveness and $\sat_{\ell_i}(u_{0,k,i})=u_{0,k,i}$ give
$|\delta_{k,i}|\le|\beta\ell_i s_{k,i}|\le\beta\ell_i$.
The recovery statements follow by substitution.
\end{proof}
With $\beta=0$ the same allocator and state give the same actuator output.
The proposition establishes neither state safety, rotor headroom,
stability, nor a bound on tracking improvement; a bounded adversarial
correction can still degrade or destabilize a nonlinear plant.

\begin{proposition}[Decoder bound and restricted contraction]
\label{prop:pes}
If $W_0\in\mathcal W$, then \eqref{eq:pes} keeps $W_k\in\mathcal W$.
If, additionally, the target and a nonzero activity $\bm a$ are fixed,
leak is zero, and readout clipping and projection remain inactive, then
\begin{equation}
 \bm\varepsilon_{k+1}
       =(1-\eta\|\bm a\|_2^2)\bm\varepsilon_k.
 \label{eq:contraction}
\end{equation}
For a nonzero initial error, its norm contracts to zero when
$0<\eta\|\bm a\|_2^2<2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Projection gives the first result. Under the additional conditions,
$(W_{k+1}-W_k)\bm a=\eta\bm\varepsilon_k\|\bm a\|_2^2$;
subtracting this from the fixed target error gives
\eqref{eq:contraction}. The scalar multiplier has modulus less than one
under the stated condition.
\end{proof}
This is the constant-feature PES result \cite{voelker2015pes}, not a
closed-loop convergence theorem. Since $\|\bm a\|^2\le2m$, $0<\eta<1/m$
suffices for that restricted model; changing targets, loss of excitation,
clipping, and leakage prevent direct application to the experiment, and
one fixed regressor does not identify all decoder weights.

\subsection{Allocation-Aware Local Stability}
\label{sec:local}
Let $E=[\bm0_{3\times1},I_3]\T$, $H=E\T$, and let
$\mathcal A_T(\bm u)=HB\clip_{[0,\bm f_{\max}]}(B^{-1}[T,\bm u\T]\T)$
be the realized torque at collective $T$. For two commands at the
\emph{same state and collective}, scalar clipping has secant slopes
$d_i\in[0,1]$. Consequently,
\begin{align}
 \mathcal A_T(\bm u)-\mathcal A_T(\bm u_0)
 &=HBDB^{-1}E\bm\delta,\\
 \|\mathcal A_T(\bm u)-\mathcal A_T(\bm u_0)\|_2
 &\le L_A\beta\|\bm\ell\|_2,
 \label{eq:allocbound}
\end{align}
where $D=\operatorname{diag}(d_i)$ and
$L_A=\max_{D\in\mathcal D}\|HBDB^{-1}E\|_2$ over the set $\mathcal D$ of
sixteen diagonal matrices with entries in $\{0,1\}$; by convexity the
vertex maximum bounds all intermediate $D$. The bound holds under rotor
clipping, but it neither compares two different closed-loop trajectories
nor bounds the height error.

For a constant reference and a smooth shortest-path branch, let
$\bm x=[\bm e_q\T,\bm\omega\T]\T$ and
$K_q=\operatorname{diag}(\bm k_q)$, $K_\omega=\operatorname{diag}(\bm k_\omega)$.
Write the continuous-time attitude subsystem as
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \dot{\bm x}=A\bm x+\bm g(\bm x)+F\bm d,\\
 A=\begin{bmatrix}0&\frac12I_3\\-\bm J^{-1}K_q&-\bm J^{-1}K_\omega\end{bmatrix},
 \qquad F=\begin{bmatrix}0\\\bm J^{-1}\end{bmatrix},
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:localmodel}
\end{equation}
where $\bm d=\bm\tau_{\rm act}+\bm\tau_{\rm p}+\bm\tau_{\rm d}
 +K_q\bm e_q+K_\omega\bm\omega$ is the total deviation from ideal PD
torque, including allocation and command-saturation errors, not just the
neural request. The nonlinear remainder is
\begin{equation}
 \bm g(\bm x)=\begin{bmatrix}
 \tfrac12[(\sqrt{1-\|\bm e_q\|^2}-1)I_3+[\bm e_q]_\times]\bm\omega\\
 -\bm J^{-1}[\bm\omega]_\times\bm J\bm\omega
 \end{bmatrix}=O(\|\bm x\|^2),
 \label{eq:localremainder}
\end{equation}
with $[\bm z]_\times\bm y=\bm z\times\bm y$. Norms below use fixed
coordinates with angular rates expressed in rad/s.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional local practical stability]
\label{thm:local}
Let $\bm J,K_q,K_\omega\succ0$, let $q_d$ be constant, and use the smooth
branch in \eqref{eq:localmodel}. Choose $Q_L\succ0$ and the solution
$P\succ0$ of $A\T P+PA=-Q_L$. On $\|\bm x\|\le r<1$, suppose
$\|\bm g(\bm x)\|\le L_r\|\bm x\|^2$ and choose $r$ so that
$c=\lambda_{\min}(Q_L)-2\|P\|L_r r>0$.
Set $p_- =\lambda_{\min}(P)$, $p_+=\lambda_{\max}(P)$,
$b=\|PF\|$, and $V=\bm x\T P\bm x$. If
\begin{equation}
 V(0)\le p_-r^2,\qquad
 \|\bm d(t)\|\le\bar d<\frac{cr}{2b}\sqrt{\frac{p_-}{p_+}},
 \label{eq:localsmallness}
\end{equation}
then $V\le p_-r^2$ is forward invariant and
\begin{equation}
 V(t)\le e^{-\alpha t}V(0)+\frac{4p_+b^2}{c^2}\bar d^2,
 \qquad \alpha=\frac{c}{2p_+}.
 \label{eq:localiss}
\end{equation}
Thus attitude regulation is locally input-to-state stable; zero
perturbation gives local exponential convergence of the plant state.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The linear system has energy
$\frac12\bm\omega\T\bm J\bm\omega+\bm e_q\T K_q\bm e_q$ with derivative
$-\bm\omega\T K_\omega\bm\omega$. Its only invariant zero-derivative
trajectory is the origin, so $A$ is Hurwitz and $P\succ0$ exists.
Equations~\eqref{eq:localmodel}--\eqref{eq:localremainder} give
\[
 \dot V\le-c\|\bm x\|^2+2b\|\bm x\|\bar d
 \le-\frac c2\|\bm x\|^2+\frac{2b^2}{c}\bar d^2.
\]
On $V=p_-r^2$, $\|\bm x\|\ge r\sqrt{p_-/p_+}$, so
\eqref{eq:localsmallness} makes the first bound strictly negative.
The ellipsoid stays inside the radius-$r$ ball; integration of
$\dot V\le-\alpha V+2b^2\bar d^2/c$ gives \eqref{eq:localiss}.
\end{proof}

If the same-state baseline deviation from ideal PD is bounded by $\bar d_0$,
\eqref{eq:allocbound} permits $\bar d=\bar d_0+L_A\beta\|\bm\ell\|$ in the
theorem, \emph{provided} \eqref{eq:localsmallness} holds: a sufficient
local robustness condition, independent of the PES trajectory, not a PES
convergence proof. $A$ is numerically Hurwitz for both implemented gain
sets; the smallness condition is not certified for the experimental
$\beta=0.5$. Moving references, the $180^\circ$ sign boundary,
sampled-data/solver error (which would need its own bound within $\bm d$),
and full translational stability remain outside this result.

\paragraph{Nengo realization.}
In Nengo~4.1.0 a custom \texttt{NeuronType} exposes membrane and gate states, and direct current injection into \texttt{ensemble.neurons} bypasses NEF gain/intercept fitting; unfiltered CMG activity uses $\eta_N=\eta(2m)/h$ and Nengo error $\bm s-\bm y^*$, leak uses pre-step weights, and projection follows the simulator step. Its teaching graph adds a one-step latency, so the weight sequences differ; over ten direct/Nengo cases the maximum relative mean-error difference is $2.013\times10^{-6}$, a tested agreement, not formal equivalence.

\subsection{Sensitivity to Measurement Noise}

Roll's increases. Seed SDs are $.001$--$.101$ pp (moderate) and
$.002$--$.031$ pp (realistic): limited repeatability, not a population
confidence interval.

\subsection{Ablation: What Drives the Improvement?}\label{sec:ablation}

We separate decoder adaptation (PES), input-dependent conductance
modulation ($\chi$), and readout gain ($G$). Toggling $\chi$, which
retains the base shunt $g_0r_j^2$, is numerically indistinguishable with
the decoder mode held fixed. \emph{Fixed versus adaptive decoder.} Holding $W_k=W_0$ keeps the neuron
dynamics but reduces the readout to $\bm a^+-\bm a^-$, a bounded, stateful
image of the PD request. It still helps (X2 $0.12$--$10.27\%$, CF2
$0.06$--$6.80\%$; Table~\ref{tab:mujoco}), so fixing the decoder does not
remove \emph{all} improvement, yet it recovers at most a fifth of the PES
gain in any cell. Fig.~\ref{fig:full3_neurons} shows why, row by row.
\emph{Membrane and gate.} At $I_j=I_b$ the membrane rests at
$v_j\approx0.75<v_{\rm th}$, so
$r_j=\sigma(\alpha_h(I_b-v_{\rm th}))=\sigma(-12.5)\approx3.7\times10^{-6}$:
the gate is deep in its logistic tail, not closed. Once $r_j\ge1/2$,
$\gamma=2$ and $v_j\ge0$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:states}) give
$b_{j,k}\ge1/2$ in \eqref{eq:gate_step}, so an opened gate cannot close
(none did in the twenty runs), and the shunt holds $v_j$ near $0.09$.
\emph{Activity.} With $G=10^5$ the resting gate already gives
$a_j\approx0.37$, graded by the input; a latched gate pins $a_j=1$. With
the fixed decoder only the $\pm y$ pair latches on Full3 (both models), so
$a^+_y-a^-_y=0$ and the pitch residual is exactly zero after 1\,s while
the sub-threshold units carry roll and yaw. Under PES on X2,
$\bm a_k\equiv\bm1$ from $2.02$\,s (all gates latched by $2.4$\,s), and
for constant $\bar{\bm a}$ with inactive projection the direct update
gives $\bm z_k=W_k\bar{\bm a}$ the recursion
\begin{equation}
 \bm z_{k+1}=(1-\lambda_W)\bm z_k
 +\eta\|\bar{\bm a}\|^2[\bm y_k^*-\clip_{[-1,1]}(\bm z_k)],
 \label{eq:constantbasis}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Neuron-Family Comparison}
The Nengo benchmark covers two models, five missions, and eleven controllers: PD and fixed/PES variants of CMG, LIF, ALIF, Izhikevich \cite{izhikevich2003}, and LIFRate, all sharing the bounded residual, saturation, and allocation interface; only the neuron dynamics and native-state-to-activity map differ.
Fig.~\ref{fig:mech_dashboard} shows two patterns. First, PES is the
dominant lever in every family: fixed decoders rarely match their PES
counterparts, even with more expressive dynamics, extending
Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation} to every basis. Second, lower error is not free:
some Izhikevich--PES cells reach the smallest error, but at an average
normalized residual RMS near 0.37 versus 0.12 for CMG--PES and with larger
decoder movement, so equal output caps do not imply equal realized effort.
Sparse LIF/ALIF activity in some cells limits the effective basis here,
which can blunt learning without implying those families cannot learn
under other encodings or gains. CMG--PES thus
attains comparable tracking at markedly lower realized effort and more
conservative decoder motion.

\subsection{Disturbance Rejection and Retention}
\label{sec:disturbance}
An equilibrium test applies
$\bm\tau_{\rm d}=d_b\rho(t)[\sin(1.31t+.2),\allowbreak
.8\sin(1.73t+1.1),\allowbreak.6\sin(2.17t+2)]\T$ in body axes.

amplitude, not a vector-norm bound.
The four-burst 45-s experiment (Fig.~\ref{fig:hover_int}) cuts burst-phase
mean error from $11.50^\circ$ to $6.65^\circ$ ($42.2\%$) and post-burst
quiet error from $1.21^\circ$ to $.99^\circ$ ($18.0\%$).
The CF2 rerun uses $d_b=.00015$ N\,m (a selected scaling, not an exact
inertia-ratio match); its full-run mean falls from
$.141199$ to $.070259$ rad ($50.24\%$).

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% [v7] Rewritten: states the positive result once and plainly, keeps the
% limits in one place instead of after every sentence, and separates the
% analog outlook from the claims.
A bounded residual of six continuous CMG units and eighteen PES-adapted decoder weights augments a fixed quaternion PD baseline without changing its gains or actuator interface, reducing mean quaternion-angle error by $17.3$--$60.6\%$ over ten matched 45-s MuJoCo cases on two modeled platforms, within rotor saturation and with exact baseline recovery at zero authority; direct and Nengo realizations agree to $2.013\times10^{-6}$ relative mean error.

Decoder adaptation is the dominant source of improvement in the tested regime: a fixed decoder gives at most $10.3\%$, and toggling $\chi$ is indistinguishable. Opened gates latch and saturate the readout, where a fixed paired decoder can cancel an axis, while sub-threshold units stay in a graded logistic tail; PES shapes the correction on either basis. Across all five neuron families PES is the consistent differentiator, and the dynamics mainly set an effort--accuracy tradeoff. The readout gain $G$, which sets the resting activity at $0.37$, is a key sensitivity parameter.

Limitations: results are in simulation (Sec.~\ref{sec:noise} bounds noise sensitivity; model mismatch and hardware effects are not addressed), the retention horizon is limited, and bounded commands, states, and weights are implementation properties, not global stability proofs.

Future work includes baselines with desired-rate feedforward and integral action, matched-effort comparisons against non-neural bounded bases, sweeps of $G$ and $\chi$, longer retention studies, held-out evaluations with frozen learned weights, full 6-DOF extension, and, most importantly, validation on physical aerial, ground, and other robotic platforms; the same bounded-residual wrapper extends to nonlinear and optimal-control baselines with hard actuator constraints.
