F. Saturation-Aware Gating on Hardstep
Hardstep commands 63◦–111◦attitude changes every 2
s, and 97–99% of its mean error accumulates during slew.
At ρ = 3 the ungated residual degrades it by 17.0% on X2
and, at β = .5, diverges on CF2 (mean error 136◦, rotors
at a bound in 92% of samples). Because the reference is
piecewise constant, the latched decoder acts as the first-order
filter (30) and lags a 12 rad/s baseline; on a saturated axis
that lag can only oppose u0 (Sec. III). At β = .5 the gate
(28) blocks the residual in 20.5% (X2) and 15.5% (CF2)
of Hardstep samples and in at most 0.04% elsewhere, so
the other eight cells differ from the ungated residual by at
most 0.1%. It cuts the X2 loss from 17.0% to 2.0% and,
noise-free, removes the CF2 divergence at β = .5 (−1.2%
on Hardstep, 36.5% geometric mean).
The gate does not make the residual beat the baseline
here, and Fig. 3 shows why. The gated residual helps while
braking (0.25–0.75 s after a step; −0.13◦of the X2 45-
s mean) but leaves a larger tail once the baseline has left
its box (+0.40◦over 0.75–1.5 s), because the decoder still
holds the slew-phase correction, with the 9.6 rad/s corner
of Sec. VI-C. That tail occurs on an unsaturated axis, so
no rule conditioned on saturation can remove it. An X2-
only exploratory screen of twelve gate configurations (load
thresholds .001–1, with and without freezing the decoder
on gated axes) ranged from −2.1% to +0.3%: at the limit
the gate blocks 79% of samples and recovers the baseline’s
settled error, but parity, not gain, is its ceiling here.
VII. CONCLUSIONS AND FUTURE WORK
Six continuous CMG units with an eighteen-parameter
PES-adapted decoder form a bounded residual that aug-
ments a quaternion PD controller without changing its
sensing, collective-thrust, allocation, or actuator interface.
The interface bounds and exact zero-authority recovery hold
independently of the learned weights, and the saturation-
aware gate preserves both. In the numerical study, the gated
residual lowers geometric-mean error against the bandwidth-
tuned PD+feedforward baseline by 37.3% on X2 and 22.4%
on CF2 noise-free, lowers burst-phase error under repeated
torque disturbances, and keeps its benefit under sensor noise
only where the baseline error exceeds the noise floor; the
ablations identify online decoder adaptation and persistent
activity (necessary, but not sufficient for low effort) as the
principal mechanisms in the tested regime. Three limitations
remain. On Hardstep the gate restores near-parity but cannot
remove the settling-tail lag of the latched decoder, which oc-
curs on an unsaturated axis. On CF2 under noise, rotor-level
saturation defeats a gate defined on the torque box and caps
the usable authority at β = .2. And the noise-free geometric
mean overstates the practical benefit, being dominated by
missions whose baseline error is already near the noise floor.
The present results are simulation based and do not
provide a closed-loop stability certificate for the reported
tracking experiments. Future work will add decoder-side lag
compensation and an allocation-aware gate; evaluate under
model mismatch; sweep the activity gain G and conductance
modulation χ more systematically; test retention with frozen
weights and held-out missions; extend the wrapper, already
confirmed across five classical control families in the plane,
to 6-DOF; and validate the method on hardware.
