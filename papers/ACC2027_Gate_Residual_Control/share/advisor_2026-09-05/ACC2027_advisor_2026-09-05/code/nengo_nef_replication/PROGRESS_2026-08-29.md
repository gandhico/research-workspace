# Progress: Nengo/NEF replication, 2026-08-29

## Interactive GUI addendum

A Nengo GUI has been added without modifying the frozen matched campaign. It
runs synchronized MuJoCo PD and PD+CMG--PES twins and displays the full signal
path: quaternion tracking, attitude DOFs, baseline/residual/final torques,
paired currents, membrane voltage, gate, decoder activity, PES decoder norms,
and rotor allocation. `launch_gui.py` fixes the backend step at 0.5 ms. The
only GUI-specific numerical change is a 1 microsecond torque-input synapse on
both twins to make the closed feedback graph schedulable; it is symmetric and
is not used for the paper metrics. The complete operator procedure and commands
are in `GUI_GUIDE_2026-08-29.md`.

The repeatable `smoke_test_gui.py` check passed for X2/hard45 and the CF2 rotor
design/yaw360. Each case ran 400 steps (0.2 s), retained finite plant,
controller, neuron and decoder signals, and verified the two-error, six-unit,
and 3-by-6 decoder schemas. Results are stored in
`results/gui_smoke_test.json`. No hardware interface was accessed.

The launcher now also provides the opt-in `--mujoco-viewer neural|baseline`,
`--realtime`, and `--viewer-fps` controls. This keeps a single authoritative
Nengo/MuJoCo process: both twins retain the 2 kHz physics/controller step while
one selected plant is drawn in a passive MuJoCo window at 30 Hz by default.
The native Windows lifecycle test passed for X2/hover: open, 400 synchronized
steps, real-time pacing, and clean close. Headless operation remains the default
and the controller equations and campaign results are unchanged.

## Outcome

The current online-PES CMG residual controller has been reproduced in Nengo
4.1.0 and tested in closed loop with MuJoCo on both paper UAVs. All six missions
were run for 45 s with matched classical baselines. The Nengo results reproduce
the archived direct implementation to within 0--2.013 parts per million in mean
quaternion error across every nontrivial mission.

This is a successful numerical replication, not merely a qualitative similarity.

## Exact signal path

```text
mission reference q_d(t)
        |
        v
shortest-path quaternion error e_q ---- measured body rate omega
        |                                      |
        +----------- quaternion PD ------------+
                         |
                 baseline torque tau_0
                         |
          normalize and split +/- per axis
                         |
                six CMG currents J
                         |
            coupled membrane v and gate r
                         |
              activity a = clip(g_r r)
                         |
       Nengo 3x6 decoder W with online PES <--- signed target y*
                         |
             normalized residual y = W a
                         |
       delta_tau = beta diag(tau_max) clip(y)
                         |
       tau = sat(tau_0 + delta_tau, tau_max)
                         |
          collective-thrust + rotor mixer
                         |
                    MuJoCo UAV
```

MuJoCo remains the plant and actuator truth. Nengo is stepped once per MuJoCo
control step at 0.5 ms. The plant state is fed to the same quaternion-PD law;
the Nengo network only generates the additive residual.

## Equations

The shortest-path quaternion error is

\[
q_e=q_d\otimes q^{-1},\qquad
e_q=\operatorname{sgn}(q_{e,0})\,\operatorname{vec}(q_e).
\]

The unchanged baseline is

\[
\tau_0=\operatorname{sat}_{\tau_{\max}}
\left(-K_q e_q-K_\omega\omega\right),
\]

with

\[
K_q=2J\,\operatorname{diag}(\omega_n^2),\qquad
K_\omega=2(0.95)J\,\operatorname{diag}(\omega_n),\qquad
\omega_n=[4,4,3.5]^\top.
\]

For normalized baseline command

\[
x_i=\tau_{0,i}/\tau_{\max,i},
\]

the paired currents are

\[
J_i^+=I_b+I_g\max(x_i,0),\qquad
J_i^-=I_b+I_g\max(-x_i,0),
\]

where `I_b=0.75` and `I_g=1.25`.

Each Nengo CMG unit evolves as

\[
\dot r=\frac{\sigma\!\left(\alpha[v-(v_{th}-\gamma r)]\right)-r}{\tau_r},
\]

\[
g(J,r)=\left(g_0+g_1|J|\right)r^2,
\]

\[
\dot v=\frac{v_{rest}-v+J}{\tau}+g(J,r)(v_{reset}-v),
\]

with `tau=0.05 s`, `tau_r=0.005 s`, `alpha=50`, `gamma=2`,
`g0=120`, and `g1=30`. The simulation uses the same stable exponential
membrane update as the canonical NumPy implementation. Decoder activity is

\[
a=\operatorname{clip}(10^5r,0,1).
\]

Thus both membrane potential and gate are essential: `v` shifts the sigmoid
argument that opens `r`, and `r` both feeds back into the threshold and changes
membrane conductance. The decoder reads the gate-derived activity, not `v`
alone and not a spike train.

The signed PES target is

\[
y^*=\operatorname{clip}(-k_e e_q-k_\omega\omega,-1,1),
\qquad k_e=8,\quad k_\omega=0.
\]

The desired paper update is

\[
W_{k+1}=\Pi_{[-6,6]}\!\left[
W_k+\eta(y^*-y)a^\top-\lambda W_k
\right],\qquad y=Wa,
\]

where `eta=8e-4` and `lambda=1e-6` per step.

Nengo implements PES as

\[
\Delta W=-\eta_N\frac{\Delta t}{N}(y-y^*)a^\top.
\]

With `N=6` and `dt=5e-4 s`, exact coefficient matching requires

\[
\eta_N=\frac{N\eta}{\Delta t}=\frac{6(8\times10^{-4})}{5\times10^{-4}}=9.6.
\]

The value 9.6 is therefore not a more aggressive physical learning rate; it is
Nengo's parameterization of the same `8e-4` discrete update. Decoder leak and
the `[-6,6]` safety projection are applied immediately after Nengo's PES step.

Finally,

\[
\delta\tau=\beta\,\operatorname{diag}(\tau_{\max})
\operatorname{clip}(y,-1,1),\qquad
\tau=\operatorname{sat}_{\tau_{\max}}(\tau_0+\delta\tau),
\]

with `beta=0.5`. This remains residual augmentation, not neural blending.

## Interpretation of the neuron plots

In Hard45 and the other amplified-gate runs, most `r` channels transition to one
and remain there. The activity therefore also saturates at one. Membrane voltage
continues to evolve because conductance and input current remain dynamic, but the
large performance improvement is not caused by rich ongoing gate modulation.
It is caused primarily by online evolution of the decoder rows. The Nengo plots
make this explicit: gate/activity curves flatten while decoder norms and applied
residual torques continue to change.

This supports the existing paper claim about the amplified-gate regime. It does
not, by itself, prove that the CMG dynamics are superior to LIF/ALIF/Izhikevich;
that claim still requires a matched neuron-family ablation inside this same
Nengo runner.
