This work studies a different arrangement: a classical

closed loop, while a low-dimensional neuromorphic dynamical system generates a bounded correction. The neuron

control demand is encoded as paired currents, processed by
stateful neuron-inspired dynamics, and decoded through
local online learning. The applied output is a continuous
gate state rather than a counted spike train; no claim of
event-only computation or neuromorphic-hardware energy
efficiency is made.
The principal contributions are:
1. a baseline-preserving neuromorphic gate-state residual
controller with explicit channel bounds and actuator
allocation;
2. a clean experimental separation between adaptive neuronal conductance and online PES decoder adaptation;
3. a rotor-actuator-faithful Skydio X2 result with more
than 50% nominal Hard45 error reduction and zero
actuator saturation;
4. a transparent Crazyflie authority study that separates
an infeasible stock virtual-moment interface from a
declared four-rotor propulsion design; and
5. a cross-backend QLabs transfer experiment that documents safe bounded execution and a negative nominalperformance result rather than overstating generality.
The current draft intentionally restricts its main performance claims to the multirotor simulations. QCar vision,
physical 3DOF Hover control, and frozen- weight generalization are future experiments and are not presented as
completed evidence.

This paper presents an online-adaptive neuromorphic gatestate residual architecture for actuator-constrained quaternion attitude control. A classical quaternion proportional–
derivative controller supplies a bounded stabilizing torque
command. Positive/negative demand pairs drive continuous HyperLIF gate states, and a projected Prescribed
Error Sensitivity (PES) rule adapts a linear decoder online. The decoded term is applied as a bounded additive residual, so the classical command is retained rather
than attenuated or replaced. All canonical multirotor
commands pass through the modeled actuator interface
and, for rotor-actuated platforms, an explicit collectivethrust/body-moment mixer. In a 45-s MuJoCo Hard45
attitude mission, the online residual reduces mean quaternion error on a Skydio X2 rotor-actuator model from
0.2941 to 0.1380 (53.1%) with zero rotor saturation. In
an explicitly labeled Crazyflie four-rotor authority design study with 1.5× nominal per-rotor thrust, it reduces
the error from 0.2854 to 0.1323 (53.6%), also without
saturation. The stock Crazyflie XML moment limits saturate for 99.88% of the mission and cannot support the
same claim. Fixed and adaptive-conductance HyperLIF
variants are numerically indistinguishable in the present
amplified-gate regime, whereas online decoder adaptation
accounts for the large improvement. A matched QUBE
Servo 3 QLabs experiment further shows that the same
controller code executes at 500 Hz with bounded voltage
and zero reported faults, while providing nominal accuracy
equivalent to LQR. These results motivate a transparent
neuromorphic controller–actuator co-design methodology
and identify the remaining robustness, frozen-weight, and
hardware-validation requirements.

7.1

Skydio X2 rotor-actuator result

Robustness and generalization protocol

The prepared five-seed robustness matrix adds a 10-degree
initial attitude, sinusoidal plant disturbance torque, attitude and rate measurement noise, and seeds 1–5. A
final submission must also include a frozen-weight protocol: train or adapt on one trajectory, freeze W , reset the
plant, and evaluate on held-out frequencies, amplitudes,
and initial conditions. Until that experiment is complete,
the paper claims online adaptation on the evaluation trajectory rather than zero-shot generalization.

Conductance versus decoder adaptation

8

Discussion

8.1

What is genuinely neuromorphic?

The architecture is neuromorphic because paired currents
drive stateful nonlinear units whose gate and membrane
variables obey neuron-inspired dynamics, and because
a local activity/error outer product adapts the decoder
online. It is not currently event driven end to end: sensors
are sampled periodically, gate states are continuous, and
MuJoCo/QLabs advance on conventional time steps. A
future event-camera implementation could convert temporal visual contrast into sparse ON/OFF events and feed
the same paired-channel principle, but such a result is
outside the present evidence.

The fixed/adaptive-conductance pairs are numerically
identical on both rotor-actuated platforms. Mean onlinerun gate state is approximately 0.963; post-gate amplification therefore places much of the activity near clipping. In
that regime, changing membrane conductance has little
influence on the decoded basis. Online PES, however,
changes W and produces the large improvement. The
current evidence supports the statement “decoder adaptation matters under this parameterization,” not “adaptive HyperLIF conductance causes the improvement.” A
gate-gain/current-bias sensitivity study is needed to characterize regimes where conductance adaptation remains
observable.

Why online PES can improve the
drones

The fixed decoder begins as a directional signed mapping
of the baseline demand. PES then reshapes that mapping
according to tracking error, allowing cross-channel combinations of the six activities. Because W is 3 × 6, each
output torque may learn from every positive/negative
axis activity rather than only its own pair. This permits
coupled residual structure while the final torque and rotor
commands remain bounded. The improvement may include trajectory-specific compensation; frozen-weight holdout evaluation is required to distinguish learned reusable
structure from online trajectory adaptation.

10

An online-adaptive neuromorphic gate-state residual can
improve aggressive quaternion attitude tracking while
retaining a bounded classical control backbone and explicit actuator allocation. On the X2 rotor model and
an explicitly labeled CF2 propulsion-authority design,
online PES reduces nominal Hard45 quaternion error
by approximately 53% without rotor saturation. The
large improvement is associated with decoder adaptation,
whereas adaptive HyperLIF conductance is a null ablation in the present amplified-gate regime. The stock CF2
result shows that actuator authority must be evaluated
together with the controller. A matched QLabs pendulum
experiment demonstrates safe cross-backend execution
but no nominal accuracy benefit. Full robustness, frozenweight hold-out, identified propulsion parameters, and
carefully staged hardware experiments remain necessary
before broader generalization claims.
