## Interpretation (exploratory; written after the results)

Figure: `pilot_idgc_multipliers.png` / `.pdf` (`experiments/six_dof_pilot/plot_pilot.py`).
The quadrature-bias prediction was written before the runs (`experiments/six_dof_pilot/PREDICTION_dither_bias.md`, PLAN addendum).

1. **G8 passes.** The P5 cascade reproduces the ACC 2027 FF6 selected classical cells bit-for-bit on both vehicles and
   all 4 missions, so every difference below comes from the inner-loop change.
2. **On the tuned cascade the attitude loop has almost no room left.**
   - Mean attitude error is 0.12–0.67°, rotors never saturate, and z_min is 0.75 m everywhere.
   - The 3-channel attitude residual (FF6 β) changes moving-mission position error by ≤ 1%: X2 geometric mean 0.994,
     CF2 1.000.
   - The one visible effect: it damps the X2 high-bandwidth hover limit cycle, 1.26 → 1.07 mm.
   - The gated v2 tuner stays at m = 1 (range ≤ 1.00–1.09), because its targets are scaled for errors near 0.1 rad
     and the tuned loop never gets there.
   - Inference, not tested here: the 13–25% FF6 CMG improvement is carried by the position channels of the
     6-channel augmentor. A position-only vs attitude-only ablation is the obvious next 6-DOF test.
3. **The closed-loop bias prediction is confirmed, and the bias is self-reinforcing.**
   - The quadrature estimate on CF2 gives ŝ = (0.33, 0.56–0.76, 0.83) where the truth is 1, raising the gains to
     (1.92–1.97, 1.21–1.52, 1.13).
   - Roll ends below the static prediction (0.62) because the bias grows with the gain it causes; the roll curve in the
     figure accelerates.
   - Even so, performance cost is small here (X2 +5%; CF2 ≈ 0) because the loop is far from saturation, but the
     mechanism is unsafe.
4. **The known-controller (indirect) estimate works where the axis model holds.**
   - CF2: ŝ = 0.95–1.01, final multipliers 0.98–1.03, consistency check Re(1/H)/(m k_ω) = 0.96–0.97 on all missions.
     Waypoint pitch is the one transient outlier (check 0.77).
   - X2 yaw: ŝ = 0.42 (truth 0.42), check 0.92; m climbs toward 2.38 but τ_id = 5 s is not finished in 15 s.
   - X2 roll/pitch fail: ŝ = 0.87–1.30 and 0.68–0.70, where the truth is 1.66 and 1.44. The consistency check flags
     this (0.09–0.37, not ≈ 1), so the single-axis second-order model does not describe the X2 roll/pitch response
     at 9–11 Hz under the stiff loop.
   - Candidates, not yet tested:
     - off-diagonal body inertia (J_xz = −0.0021) coupling the stiff roll loop into the soft, mis-scaled yaw loop;
     - outer-loop coupling through the ω_des feedforward;
     - the mixer's moment arms (thrust sites at z = 0.05 and 0.08).
   - The check is the useful part. A post-hoc reading on the last-5 s means:
     - a validity gate of |check − 1| < 0.2 would pass the correct estimates (CF2 except waypoint pitch; X2 yaw);
     - it would block the wrong ones: X2 roll/pitch, and CF2 waypoint pitch, where ŝ = 1.97.
     - This is not yet tested as an online gate.
5. **The raised gains hurt the X2 hover limit cycle:** 1.26 → 2.5–3.0 mm. This agrees with the FF6 caveat that the
   tuned X2 sits near a high-bandwidth limit cycle.

**Idea of 6-DOF behaviour, in one line.** In a well-tuned, noise-free cascade the attitude-level dual adds nothing
measurable. The value of learning has to come from the position channels, or from conditions with room to improve
(noise, disturbance, mismatch, aggressive references). Identification-based correction needs the known-controller form
plus a consistency gate before it is safe on real vehicles.
