## Interpretation (written after compiling, 2026-09-12)

**Gates.** G3 passes: two v1 cells rerun bit-identically.

### Pre-declared claim (i): admissibility with z_min > 0.2 m

- **CF2: holds.** All four v2 controllers are admissible on all seven missions.
- **X2: fails, but the rule does not discriminate on X2.** It flags the PID baseline itself, and the ACC 2027 residual, on both step missions.

Contact analysis (`check_ground_contact.py`, `contact_detail.py`):
- On X2 Hardstep, the paper's PD baseline grazes the floor with `rotor1` for **2 steps (1 ms) at t = 7.1 s**, with 0.11 mm penetration.
- PID k_i = 0.05 does the same on Hardstep (1 ms) and on Hardstep_b (0.5 ms at 3.58 s).
- Across all 30 X2 step cells, z_min lies between 0.080 and 0.202 m, a band that includes PID (0.084–0.118 m).

**Post hoc classification (labelled; not a pre-declared rule).** "Crash" means sustained ground contact, z_min < 0.05 m. The data separate cleanly: every crash cell has z_min ≤ 0.017 m, every other cell has z_min ≥ 0.080 m.
- There are **10 crash cells**. All are CF2 step missions under v0/v1 gain-raising tuners (st_pid, st_pid_f, dual, dual_f, stn_b_ks, dual_stn_b_ks).
- **v2 controllers crash in 0 of 56 cells.**

### Claims (ii) and (iii): hold on both vehicles

| | X2 | CF2 |
|---|---|---|
| dual_v2 / pid_res, smooth (original geo / held-out Hard45b) | 0.969 / 0.960 | 0.951 / 0.940 |
| dual_v2 / pid_res, geo over all 7 | **0.976** | **0.963** |
| dual_stn_ks_v2 / pid_res, all 7 | 0.977 | 0.964 |

### The price of safety

Gating removed most of the tuner's own benefit:
- The tuner alone went from st_pid v0 at 0.787 (X2) to st_pid_v2 at 0.921 (X2) and 0.884 (CF2).
- Dual's advantage over the residual alone shrank from about 11% (v0 dual, smooth, X2: 0.467 vs 0.524) to 3–5%.
- The dual architecture is now **safe on both vehicles and consistently, but modestly, better than the ACC 2027 residual.**

### Where the self-tuning gain goes

On smooth missions the v2 tuner raises m_p and m_i by 5–35% (the STN variant up to 1.5), and damping stays at nominal. On step missions the validity gate keeps gains near nominal during the large transients.

### Status of the published law (Olivares et al. 2026) for attitude control

- As written (unbounded, learned sensitivity), it is inadmissible.
- It becomes safe and mildly useful only with (a) the known sensitivity sign, (b) bounds and leak, and (c) authority and validity gating.
- Items (a)–(c) are this project's contribution to that line of work.

### v3 directions (to be declared before any run)

1. **Fix the cause, not the tuner.** Thrust-priority allocation (scale torque to preserve collective within rotor limits) for all controllers. This changes the plant interface, so PID and the residual rerun as new baselines.
2. **Sensitivity identified from residual-channel excitation.** The residual R perturbs the command independently of the PID error, avoiding the closed-loop sign bias of §6c. This is a genuine dual-architecture coupling.
3. **Exploratory sensitivity of e_gate and the headroom definition,** with a new held-out mission pair.
4. **6-DOF** (Phase 2), where altitude and attitude are controlled jointly.
5. **Nengo realization** of the v2 tuners (backend conformance, as in ACC 2027).

### Note for the ACC 2027 paper

- **What happens.** In the X2 Hardstep cell, the PD baseline descends to about 0.12 m and one rotor touches the floor for 1 ms (0.1 mm penetration). The CMG–PES run reaches z_min 0.169 m.
- **Why it doesn't matter.** A 1 ms contact on a 45 s run cannot change the mean error meaningfully, but it is a physical event in the published cell.
- **Options:**
  - a one-clause disclosure: "X2 Hardstep descends to 0.12 m with a 1 ms rotor-floor graze";
  - or a rerun from a higher start altitude for the journal version.
